The proposition

“The world is losing its forests.”

Source: IMF Climate Change Indicators (FAO forest indicators), 1992–2023. The charts below use forest area and (on the opposing side) forest carbon stocks.

1 | In support

Forest area has decreased, and some countries lost much more than others.

Two complementary views show the worldwide trend and the different scales of national forest loss.

Global forest area has fallen since 1992

A global total frames the change in absolute terms, showing the amount of forest lost.

Design decisions and rationale
  • Truncated y-axis (−1): Beginning at 3,900M hectares makes a 4.2% decline occupy more visual space; the axis is disclosed in the source line.
  • Prominent absolute-loss annotation (−1): Directs attention to 176.81M hectares rather than the smaller-looking percentage.
  • Red line and shaded region (−0.5): Emphasizes loss through a consistent visual metaphor.
  • Full 1992–2023 window (+1.5): Gives long-term context without selecting only the steepest years.

Smaller countries can lose a larger share of forests

Bar chart report the percentage lost and label the absolute loss.

Design decisions and rationale
  • Selected countries (−1): The three largest absolute-loss cases and two largest percentage-loss cases highlight decline, excluding gainers.
  • Indexing 1992 to 100 (+1.5): Reduces the confounding effect of country size when comparing trends.
  • Line + bar in one figure (+2): Shows how each country's forest changed over time and how much it lost.
  • Natural sequential reds (−0.5): Darker shades emphasize larger proportional losses.
  • Percentage and hectare labels (+1.5): Supplies both relative and absolute context.
